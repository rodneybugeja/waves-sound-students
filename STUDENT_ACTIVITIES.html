Activity Lab

These are designed for mixed-ability 13–14-year-olds. The rule is predict → test → evidence → explain. Do not skip the prediction: being wrong and then changing your mind is part of the physics.

Activity 1Foundations8 min

Human Wave: Energy Moves, People Stay

What you need

Open space; 8–12 students; one coloured wristband or sticky note.

Student mission

  1. Student 1 raises and lowers both hands once.
  2. Each student copies only when the neighbour’s hands rise.
  3. Repeat faster, then reverse direction.
  4. Track the marked student: they move locally but do not travel with the pulse.

Think like a physicist

  • Predict: will the marked student reach the other end?
  • What moved across the line: people, energy, or both?
  • What would have to change for matter to be transferred?

Record

Write your prediction first, then your observation/evidence, then your explanation. If your prediction was wrong, explain what changed your mind.

Slinky Particle Detective

What you need

Long slinky; masking tape; floor markers.

Student mission

  1. Make a transverse pulse by moving one end sideways once.
  2. Watch only the taped coil.
  3. Make a longitudinal pulse by pushing/pulling along the slinky.
  4. Students sketch particle motion and energy direction for both.

Think like a physicist

  • Predict the taped coil’s motion before each pulse.
  • What evidence makes one wave transverse and the other longitudinal?
  • If the pulse travels right, must the coil travel right?

Activity 3Foundations10 min

Frequency–Period Beat Challenge

What you need

Stopwatch; metronome app optional; mini-whiteboards.

Student mission

  1. Teacher gives a steady beat for 10 s.
  2. Pairs count cycles and estimate frequency.
  3. Use T = 1/f to predict period.
  4. Check by timing 5 or 10 cycles and dividing.

Think like a physicist

  • Which is more reliable: timing one cycle or ten? Why?
  • If frequency doubles, what happens to period?
  • Can two waves have the same period but different frequency?

Record

Write your prediction first, then your observation/evidence, then your explanation. If your prediction was wrong, explain what changed your mind.

Activity 21Foundations10 min

Wave Label Relay

What you need

Mini-whiteboards or A4 paper; coloured pens; slide 19/video.

Student mission

  1. Watch 30-60 seconds of the wave-properties video.
  2. Teacher pauses; teams draw one wave from memory.
  3. Label crest, trough, equilibrium, amplitude and wavelength.
  4. Swap boards and check another team.

Think like a physicist

  • Why is amplitude measured from the equilibrium line?
  • Why can wavelength be crest-to-crest OR trough-to-trough?
  • Which quantity cannot be found from one frozen picture alone: frequency or wavelength?

Record
