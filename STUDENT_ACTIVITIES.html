Activity Lab

These are designed for mixed-ability 13–14-year-olds. The rule is predict → test → evidence → explain. Do not skip the prediction: being wrong and then changing your mind is part of the physics.

Activity 21Foundations10 min

Wave Label Relay

What you need

Mini-whiteboards or A4 paper; coloured pens; slide 19/video.

Student mission

  1. Watch 30-60 seconds of the wave-properties video.
  2. Teacher pauses; teams draw one wave from memory.
  3. Label crest, trough, equilibrium, amplitude and wavelength.
  4. Swap boards and check another team.

Think like a physicist

  • Why is amplitude measured from the equilibrium line?
  • Why can wavelength be crest-to-crest OR trough-to-trough?
  • Which quantity cannot be found from one frozen picture alone: frequency or wavelength?

Record

Correct one label in a different colour and explain the correction.

Activity 22Ripple tank12 min

Ripple Tank Pattern Hunt

What you need

Slide 39 virtual ripple tank; worksheet or mini-whiteboard.

Student mission

  1. Predict the pattern from a straight dipper.
  2. Switch to a point source and sketch the new pattern.
  3. Increase frequency while depth stays fixed.
  4. Pause and compare wavefront spacing.

Think like a physicist

  • What does each blue line represent?
  • Why do closer wavefronts mean shorter wavelength?
  • What evidence shows the source shape affects the wavefront shape?

Record

Draw both patterns and write one evidence-based rule.

Activity 23Ripple tank8 min

Human Wavefront and Ray

What you need

Masking tape or floor line; 5-8 students; one arrow card.

Student mission

  1. Students stand shoulder-to-shoulder as one wavefront.
  2. Another student holds the energy-direction arrow.
  3. Rotate the wavefront; place the ray at 90°.
  4. Check against slide 40.

Think like a physicist

  • Is a ray a physical line in the water?
  • What information does the ray represent?
  • Why must it be perpendicular to a plane wavefront?

Record

Sketch three differently oriented wavefronts and add the correct ray to each.

Activity 24Ripple tank12 min

Beat the Reflection Simulator

What you need

Slide 46 reflection animation; protractor; mini-whiteboards.

Student mission

  1. Teacher hides/ignores the readout and chooses an incidence angle.
  2. Teams predict r before the animation is run.
  3. Pause at the barrier and identify normal, i and r.
  4. Repeat for three angles.

Think like a physicist

  • Why are i and r measured from the normal?
  • Do the wavefronts themselves lie along the rays?
  • What stays the same after reflection?

Record

Make a three-row table of predicted r, observed r and conclusion.

Activity 25Sound12 min

Resonance Detective: Why Did the Glass Break?

What you need

Slide 78 slow-motion glass video; mini-whiteboards. No live glass-breaking demonstration required.

Student mission

  1. Predict what must match before the glass can vibrate strongly.
  2. Watch for visible flexing in slow motion.
  3. Identify the evidence for increasing vibration amplitude.
  4. Explain resonance in one sentence.

Think like a physicist

  • Would any frequency work equally well?
  • Why does repeated energy transfer matter?
  • How is resonance different from simply making the sound louder?

Record

Write Claim - Evidence - Reasoning using the slow-motion footage.
