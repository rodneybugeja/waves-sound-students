MythBusters × Waves & Sound

Use short, relevant segments as evidence-analysis lessons rather than passive video time. Students predict first, collect evidence while watching, explain the physics, then challenge the method and transfer the idea to a new situation.

Episode numbering: Season/episode numbers below follow Apple TV listings. Some broadcasters package MythBusters differently, so numbering can vary. Use your school/licensed/legal source for playback; these files contain activities and worksheets, not copyrighted episodes.
01

MYTHBUSTERS ACTIVITY 1

Breaking Glass

Season 3, Episode 13 · 2005

Resonance, natural frequency, frequency, amplitude and energy transfer by sound

A singer tries to shatter a glass using sound. It gives students a concrete way to connect vibration, frequency and resonance.

PredictCommit before the result.EvidenceRecord what is actually observed/measured.ExplainUse physics vocabulary and diagrams.EvaluateDoes the method justify the conclusion?
Sample critical-thinking questions
  1. What must be true about the sound frequency if the glass is to vibrate strongly?
  2. Is singing louder enough on its own? Explain your prediction.
  3. List every variable the team changes or controls.
  4. What evidence shows that the glass itself is vibrating before it breaks?
  5. Use the words natural frequency, resonance, amplitude and energy to explain the result.
  6. Why would two glasses that look similar not necessarily respond to the same note?

Transfer: A loudspeaker plays a note close to an object’s natural frequency. Predict what happens as the driving frequency is moved gradually away from resonance, and justify your prediction.

02

MYTHBUSTERS ACTIVITY 2

Voice Flame Extinguisher

Season 5, Episode 7 · 2007

Sound as a pressure wave, amplitude, energy and interaction with matter

The episode tests whether sound can disturb air enough to extinguish a flame, making pressure variations and sound energy visible through an effect.

PredictCommit before the result.EvidenceRecord what is actually observed/measured.ExplainUse physics vocabulary and diagrams.EvaluateDoes the method justify the conclusion?
Sample critical-thinking questions
  1. Can sound move matter even though the sound wave does not carry the air across the room?
  2. Which should matter more here: frequency, amplitude, distance, or all three? Rank them and justify.
  3. What changes between the unamplified and amplified tests?
  4. What observations support the idea that sound consists of pressure variations in air?
  5. Explain how a sound wave can transfer energy without transferring the air from source to flame.
  6. Suggest two variables that should be controlled in a repeat investigation.

Transfer: Predict how moving the sound source twice as far from the flame would affect the result. State what extra evidence you would want before being confident.

03

MYTHBUSTERS ACTIVITY 3

Deep Space Hollywood

MythBusters Jr. - Season 1, Episode 8 · 2019

Sound needs a medium; particle model and sound in space

One myth asks whether anyone in space can hear you scream - a direct route into why mechanical sound waves need particles.

PredictCommit before the result.EvidenceRecord what is actually observed/measured.ExplainUse physics vocabulary and diagrams.EvaluateDoes the method justify the conclusion?
Sample critical-thinking questions
  1. If an astronaut shouts outside a spacecraft, what happens to the vocal cords? What happens to the sound?
  2. Why can light cross space but ordinary sound cannot?
  3. Which part of the demonstration models the absence of a medium?
  4. What observation is evidence that sound transmission changes when particles are removed?
  5. Explain the result using vibrations and collisions between particles.
  6. Why can astronauts communicate by radio even though they cannot hear each other directly through space?

Transfer: Imagine a bell inside a sealed jar as the air is slowly removed. Sketch and explain how the detected sound amplitude should change.
